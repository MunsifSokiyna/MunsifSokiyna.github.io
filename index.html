About

Researcher, educator, and former industry technologist

I'm an Assistant Professor of Information Systems and Operations Management in the Business Analytics, Information Systems and Supply Chain Management (BISC) Department at the University of Central Oklahoma. My research examines how people navigate trust, toxicity, and collaboration in digital communities - particularly open-source software (OSS) projects - and how generative AI is reshaping developer motivation, contribution, and coping behavior online.

I completed my PhD in Management Information Systems at the University of Alabama's Culverhouse College of Business, where I also served as instructor of record for Fundamentals of MIS, teaching roughly 150 students per section and receiving the 2025 Outstanding Doctoral Student Teaching Award. My work combines NLP, large language models, longitudinal GitHub data, and survey methods, and has appeared in venues including Behavior & Information Technology, Sustainable Computing: Informatics and Systems, AMCIS, and ICIS.

Before moving into academia, I spent nearly a decade in industry - leading IT operations for McDonald's franchise locations in Jordan and co-founding a web development studio - experience that still shapes how I teach technology in practice.

    Teaching Philosophy

    My teaching bridges theoretical concepts with practical, hands-on application - helping students understand how information systems and data-driven decision-making shape modern business operations. I integrate AI tools (ChatGPT, neural network simulators, embedding projectors) directly into course design so students build fluency with the technologies they'll actually use.
